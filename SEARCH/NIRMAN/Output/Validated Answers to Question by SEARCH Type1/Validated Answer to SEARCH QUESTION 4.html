5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Spearman Rank Correlation with t-Approximation (Tie-Corrected Baseline)

This run is the baseline and follows the user's specified analysis. Spearman is the right default for two coarse ordinal survey scales. Cause of Work is a rank_1_to_8 item, and engagement is a 5-level ordinal with an arbitrary spacing between 'Monetary contribution' and 'Fulltime as a career'. Because the schema says lower rank means stronger preference, the 9 - x inversion matters. Without it rho would come out negative. Pre N=2,001 comes from 2,155 Level-0.1 Pre rows after dropping undecided and null Y (about 154 rows). Post N=1,430 comes from 1,451 rows (about 21 dropped). Rho rises slightly from 0.25 to 0.28, but the two phases are different, overlapping sets of people (Pre has about 700 more participants), so this difference was not tested. The paired rho of 0.16 is lower because it correlates changes, which are noisy and full of ties. Over 50% of participants show no engagement change, and Pre 'Fulltime as a career' respondents cannot go higher (ceiling at 5). Levels 0.2 and 0.3 contain Post rows only, so no Pre statistics exist for those Points.

Run #2: Kendall Tau-b Concordance with Normal-Approximation Test

Kendall tau-b is computed from the 8x5 importance-by-engagement table and corrects for ties in both variables. Those ties are heavy: 8 rank levels, 5 engagement levels, and about half of respondents giving rank 1 to Cause of Work at Post. Tau-b is smaller than rho (0.21 vs 0.25 Pre; 0.24 vs 0.28 Post; paired 0.13 vs 0.16) because the two statistics measure different things. Tau-b is a probability difference between concordant and discordant pairs, while rho behaves more like Pearson on ranks. Both give the same sign and the same significance (z of 11.60 and 10.69). This confirms the association does not depend on the estimator, and that the modest strength is a property of the data. The Levels 0.2/0.3 tau-b values (0.29, 0.26) come from Post-only rows in the invitation-gated Point 2/3 subsets.

Run #3: Contingency-Table Chi-Square, Cramer's V and Mantel-Haenszel Linear-by-Linear Trend Test

This run treats the data as an 8x5 table. The trend test (M² about 103 Pre, 88 Post; r 0.23/0.25) tests the ordered hypothesis that matters here, whether more importance goes with more commitment, and agrees with the rank correlations. The omnibus chi-square is usable Pre (203.29, V=0.16, 15% of cells with E<5). Post it is sparse (35% of cells with E<5, 7 with E<1), so Run 3 correctly declined to interpret it. The sparsity is a domain artifact. After the workshop about half of participants put 'Cause of Work' at rank 1, which empties the lower-rank rows. The Level 0.3 table is even more fragile, with empty importance rows and an empty ordinal column. The top-2 vs 3-8 odds ratios (2.71 Pre, 2.92 Post) are the simple 'is cause important to you' contrast. They are consistent with the per-rank-step odds ratio of about 1.24-1.27 in Run 5. Domain Nuances & Constraints

1) Intent, not outcome: Y is a self-reported plan, and X is a stated priority. Neither is a realised result like Demographics.Current_Status_Of_Work or Person_Years_Of_Social_Action. The association shows attitudinal alignment, not career realisation. 2) Causation: the data are single-arm observational, with no control group. Pre/Post differences, including the higher Post fulltime rates, cannot be attributed to the workshop. 3) Pre and Post are different, overlapping samples: at Level 0.1 there are about 2,155 Pre rows vs 1,451 Post rows, and only about 1,450 matched pairs (1,289 with valid X and Y both times). Pre-vs-Post contrasts mix composition change (batch coverage, attrition) with change over time. The rho gap of 0.03 was not tested, and the paired analysis is the cleaner view of change. 4) Rank-scale behaviour: Life_Criteria_Cause_Of_Work is rank_1_to_8 (the theme lists 9 criteria on an 8-point scale). About half of Post respondents give it rank 1, which suggests ties or compression rather than a strict ordering. Post-workshop compression and the engagement ceiling (5 = Fulltime) restrict variance and produce heavy ties (53.53% of pairs with no engagement change). They also leave sparse cells at ranks 5-8 (rank 8 has 76 Pre, 46 Post), which explains the non-monotonic bumps (Pre rank 7, Post rank 5). Do not interpret those bumps. 4b) Cleaning: 0 means not answered, so it is set to NaN. The schema's explicit out_of_range list does not name Life_Criteria_Cause_Of_Work, but applying the 1-8 rule is harmless. 5) 'Not yet decided / confused' is mapped to NaN in the ordinal and excluded from correlations, but it is counted in the 'fulltime %' denominators. It is concentrated at low importance (22.37% at Pre rank 8), so it is informative and not random. About 175 rows were dropped for it, plus 4 true nulls. The fulltime % and mean-ordinal figures use different denominators and are not directly comparable. 6) Levels 0.2 and 0.3 are Point 2/3 workshops with Post rows only (no Pre). They are invitation-gated, self-selected subsets with very high fulltime base rates (0.72 and 0.90), so their rho values are not comparable with Level 0.1 and cannot show change over time. 7) No batch control: Workshop_Batch_Number (7-17 in the questionnaire) and Workshop_Name were not controlled. Recent batches differ in time since workshop and possibly in program emphasis. 8) Multiple rows per person across levels and phases mean that pooling phases or levels would violate independence. Keep analyses stratified, as done here. 9) P-values rely on normal approximations. Displayed zeros mean p<0.005 (or the permutation floor of about 0.0005-0.001). Report effective N after every filter.

What this means in plain language

  • Direction: Ranking Cause of Work higher goes with planning more serious involvement, from monetary giving up to a full-time social-action career.
  • Size: The link is weak to moderate. Rank explains only about 5–6% of the variation in engagement plans (r² ≈ 0.05–0.06). Many people with a high Cause-of-Work rank do not plan full-time work, and vice versa.
  • Standout group: People who put Cause of Work at rank 1 are the most likely to choose full-time (45.29% Pre, 65.06% Post). Beyond rank 1 the pattern is bumpy rather than a smooth gradient. Rank 8 groups are small, so those bumps are probably noise.
  • Size of the gap: Top-2 rankers are roughly 2× as likely to choose full-time before the workshop (37% vs 18%). After the workshop the gap is closer to 1.75× (61% vs 35%). The odds ratios of 2.71 (Pre) and 2.92 (Post) are odds, not probabilities, so they sound bigger than the percentage gap.

Business value

  1. A useful segmentation signal. The Cause-of-Work rank helps identify who is most likely to pursue social action as a career. Those who rank it 1st or 2nd are natural candidates for full-time pathway programs, fellowships, mentoring and placement support.
  2. Tailor the message by segment. Participants who rank Cause of Work lower still plan some involvement (mean engagement about 3.6–3.9 out of 5). Volunteering, part-time and contribution options probably suit them better than a push toward a full-time career.
  3. Use it for groups, not individuals. With rho ≈ 0.25–0.28, the rank is a dependable group-level signal but a poor predictor for any single person. It should not be the only criterion for selecting or screening participants.
  4. Don't expect values shifts alone to move plans. Among the 1,289 people with both Pre and Post data, a larger rise in Cause-of-Work importance was only weakly linked to a larger rise in engagement (rho = 0.16).
  5. Consistent evidence base. Five different methods (Spearman, Kendall tau-b, trend/chi-square, bootstrap/permutation, regression) all gave the same direction, significance and sample sizes. This gives high confidence in the main finding. The methods share the same data and cleaning rules, so they check the method, not the data. What not to conclude

  • No causation. This is single-arm observational data with no control group.
  • The Pre-to-Post increase can't be credited to the workshop.
  • Full-time share and importance both rose after the workshop. In the paired group, mean importance rose by 1.22 and mean engagement by 0.54.
  • Pre and Post samples differ (2,001 vs 1,430), and later participation is self-selected.
  • Engagement also rose among people whose importance didn't change, which suggests a general upward shift.
  • Don't say the link is stronger after the workshop. The gap between rho 0.25 and 0.28 is small and was not formally tested.
  • Levels 0.2 and 0.3 are Post-only.
  • Rho is 0.32 (N = 522) and 0.27 (N = 166).
  • They show a similar positive link but can't show change over time.
  • Level 0.3 is fragile (CI 0.08–0.45).

Data handling to be aware of

  • 175 "Not yet decided / confused" rows and 4 null answers were left out of the correlations. The undecided share is higher at low-importance ranks (for example 22.37% at Pre rank 8). The undecided rows are still counted in the full-time percentages as "not full-time".
  • The Post chi-square table was too sparse to interpret, so that run relied on the trend test and the rank correlations.
