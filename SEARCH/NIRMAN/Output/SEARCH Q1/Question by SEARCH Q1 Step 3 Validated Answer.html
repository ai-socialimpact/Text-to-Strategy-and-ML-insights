Domain Overview & Consensus Interpretation

NIRMAN is SEARCH's youth social-leadership programme. Its Questionnaire table (prod_nirman_questionnaire) records subjective psychometrics once per Pre/Post phase of each workshop level. The question asks whether a participant's stated post-workshop intention to pursue a social-sector career 'Fulltime as a career' (the top rung, 5, of the Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement ordinal scale) is mainly predicted by (1) where they already stood at Pre and (2) how strongly they already ranked NGO or social-entrepreneurship careers above private or government jobs. This matches the schema's own ML guidance. The questionnaire YAML defines is_fulltime and switched_to_fulltime (Post=1 and Pre=0) as recipes, and the Pre-vs-Post Point-1 shortlist names these exact career-preference fields as candidate drivers. The shortlist is explicitly 'not yet validated', so this analysis is the validation step. All five runs used Workshop_Level 0.1 (Point 1), batches 11–17, and matched about 1,450 participants. Pre full-time is 0.25 and Post full-time is 0.53. Persistence is the dominant pattern: about 0.92 of Pre full-timers stay and about 0.40 of Pre non-full-timers move. Because Pre status is part of the outcome's own history, the 'stayers' dominate the overall signal. The movers subgroup is the same population as the schema's switched_to_fulltime target and is the fairer test of the preference hypothesis. The data is single-arm observational, so every result is association, not proof that the workshop caused the change. The outcome is a self-reported plan captured immediately after the workshop. It is not the realised work status held in Demographics.Current_Status_Of_Work. The verdict 'broadly supported' is therefore a statement about stated career intent. It is not a statement about careers actually entered.

5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Parametric Baseline: Conditional Proportions, Cohen's d and Standardized Logistic Regression

This run is the reference computation. It gives N=1,450 with 365 Pre full-timers, of whom 337 stay (0.92). Of the 1,085 Pre non-full-timers, 431 move (0.40). The 337 stayers are 0.44 of the 768 Post full-timers. The Pre non-full-time group is every other plan category: Part time, Volunteering frequently, Occasionally, Monetary contribution, plus any undecided or null rows. A 0.40 mover rate is therefore an average over very different starting points. The four d values (+0.71 NGO, +0.61 SE/NGO, −0.42 private, −0.38 government) are all in the expected direction. The full-sample effects are large because the Post full-time group contains the stayers, who were already social-sector oriented at Pre. Pre preference and Pre status are two readings of the same orientation. Among movers the d values halve (0.33, 0.25, −0.21, −0.20), which is the better estimate of preference as an independent predictor. In the standardized logistic model, is_fulltime_pre has the largest coefficient (0.87) and NGO is second (0.35). Batch number (0.17) ties with SE/NGO (0.17). The CV AUC falls only from 0.77 to 0.75 without Pre status, and Pre status alone gives about 0.69. This is because the rank items are a forced ranking of 8 options, so they are partly ipsative and correlate with prior status. The median expected income at 30 is lower for full-timers (80k vs 100k). That matches the schema's secondary target 'lower income expectation', and it is consistent with social-sector career intent trading off financial expectation.

Run #2: Robust Non-Parametric: Bootstrap CIs, Medians/IQR, Mann-Whitney U with Cliff's Delta and Univariate Rank-AUC

The rank_1_to_8 items are ordinal with many ties and heavy use of the middle ranks. Cohen's d on inverted ranks therefore treats a rank gap as interval-scaled. Medians (NGO 6 vs 5), Mann-Whitney and Cliff's delta (NGO 0.39, SE/NGO 0.33, private −0.24, government −0.23) confirm the same ordering without that assumption. Participant-level bootstrap CIs (stay 0.90–0.95, mover 0.37–0.43, stayer share 0.40–0.47) show persistence is stable. The run also gives the cleanest audit of data lineage: 4,304 rows → 2,912 → 1,462 participants → 1,460 Pre / 1,452 Post → 1,450 pairs. Only about 12 participants are lost, so Pre/Post attrition is minimal. Univariate AUCs (Pre status 0.70, NGO 0.69, SE/NGO 0.67, private and government 0.38) show that NGO preference alone nearly matches Pre status on its own. This is why 'mostly' is questionable. Within movers, batch number outranks government preference, which reflects cohort-level differences in the Point-1 programme and not a preference effect. Only 1,320 respondents have a usable income value, so the income comparison rests on a smaller N, and round-number heaping in free-typed rupee entries explains the equal 100k medians among movers.

Run #3: Batch-Stratified Weighted Estimation: Mantel-Haenszel Pooling, Inverse-Variance Pooled Effect Sizes and Batch Fixed-Effects Logistic Regression

Workshop_Batch_Number is the schema's time axis (17 = most recent), and later cohorts are censored and differ in size. Matched N by batch 11–17 is 88, 96, 126, 148, 459, 359 and 174, so batches 15 and 16 contribute about 56% of the data. Stratifying tests whether the pattern is a pooled-cohort artefact. It is not: the Mantel-Haenszel OR is 19.63 against a crude 18.26, every batch shows stay rate > 0.5, and the mover rate is 0.30–0.50 in six of seven batches (batch 16 is 0.51). Batches 11 and 12 have 100% stay with no leavers, so their odds ratios rest on a continuity correction. Pooled d values (0.71, 0.64, −0.45, −0.42) are marginally larger than crude for the private and government items. This shows batch composition was masking a little of the preference contrast and not creating it. With batch fixed effects and class weights, Pre status has its largest standardized coefficient (0.98, OR 9.63). The preference effects keep their direction, with movers OR 1.36, 1.17, 0.81 and 0.75. This supports the schema's censoring guidance: control for batch. The conclusion survives the control, so it is not a recency or cohort-mix effect.

Run #4: Outlier-Trimmed and Regularized: 5% Trimmed Means, Winsorized Income, L2-Regularized Class-Weighted Logistic Regression with CV Drop-Column and Permutation Importance

Trimming 5% from each tail of a bounded 1–8 ordinal variable truncates the very tails where groups differ and shrinks the within-group SD. This is why trimmed d values (0.88, 0.74, −0.55, −0.47) are larger than the untrimmed ones (0.71 and so on). The larger values are a scaling artefact of the method, not stronger evidence. Winsorizing and clipping income at 5k–1M handles free-typed INR outliers as the schema requires. L2 regularisation with strict in-fold imputation and z-scoring shrinks and spreads weight across the correlated rank items. SE/NGO and private add almost no unique signal because the four ranks are mutually constrained: placing NGO high pushes other options down. Run #5: Rank-Order and Cumulative Importance Share: Shallow Decision Tree plus TensorFlow.js Logistic Unit with Cross-Criterion Rank Concordance

This run has a smaller sample (1,290 matched, 926 movers) because it also removed respondents who were 'not yet decided' or in invalid categories (154 at Pre, 21 at Post). The schema encodes 'Not yet decided / confused' as null/NaN for ordinal models, and the other four runs dropped only literal nulls. Excluding the undecided removes mostly Pre non-full-timers who may be potential movers. That is why the stayer share rises to 0.49, the mover rate becomes 0.38 and the base rates become 0.54 Post and 0.28 Pre. The substantive conclusions are unchanged, but the difference is a cohort-definition effect, not instability. The tree splits first on is_fulltime_pre (importance 0.85), and Pre status ranks first on all four criteria (tree, TF.js |weight|, |d|, univariate AUC). Kendall's tau of about 0.87 shows the criteria broadly agree. The Pre-to-Post transition table shows where movers come from within the ordinal engagement ladder. In movers the tree splits first on SE/NGO and gives batch number importance 0.30. Part of the movers signal therefore reflects cohort differences, not individual preference. In the TF.js model government outranks SE/NGO, which is the sort of ordering swap expected among overlapping rank items. Shallow trees with min_samples_leaf 20 are unstable at this N.

Domain Nuances & Constraints

1) Outcome construct: Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement is a self-reported plan captured at the Post phase of the Point-1 workshop. It is not realised work, which lives in Demographics.Current_Status_Of_Work and has no transition dates. A Post 'Fulltime as a career' answer may reflect workshop enthusiasm and social-desirability effects. 2) Single-arm observational data: there is no control group, so the Pre-to-Post rise (0.25 → 0.53) is change, not causal effect. The p-values and CIs quantify association only. 3) Stayers and the 'mostly' wording: Pre status is by far the strongest single feature (OR about 10–19, top rank in every model). However, CV AUC falls only from about 0.77–0.78 to about 0.75 without it, and NGO preference alone has a univariate AUC of about 0.69. The two predictors are largely redundant, both reflecting the same latent social-sector orientation, so 'mostly predicted by Pre status' is true for rank order and overstated for unique contribution. 4) Ipsative rank items: the Career_Preference items form one forced ranking (1 = first choice, 8 = last), so they are mutually dependent. This explains why Working_In_Ngo and Social_Entrepreneurship_Or_Ngo overlap, why private and government are mirror images, and why only NGO and government reliably rank next in multivariable models. The claim that all four preferences come second is only partly supported. 5) Rank scale: lower rank means stronger preference, so inversion (9 - rank) is mandatory. A missed inversion would flip every sign. The schema lists the >8 range rule for Social_Entrepreneurship_Or_Ngo, and applying it to the other three is harmless but extra. 6) Movers analysis: the effects roughly halve (|d| about 0.2–0.33) and AUC is only 0.63–0.65. This is the true test of whether preferences predict switching, and it equals the schema's switched_to_fulltime definition (Pre = 0). The ML shortlist is described as unvalidated, and these results give it only modest support. 7) Cohort and censoring: batches 15–16 hold about 56% of the pairs, batches 11–12 are small (88 and 96) with 100% stay, and batch number carries some signal in movers. Recent cohorts are censored with respect to any realised outcome. 8) Handling of undecided responses: 'Not yet decided / confused' is a null in ordinal models. Runs 1–4 keep these respondents in the Pre non-full-time group, while Run 5 excludes them. Report which definition is used. 9) Income: Financial_Expected_Income_Age_30 is free-typed, has only about 1,320 usable values, heaps at round numbers, and shows lower or equal medians among full-timers. Do not read this as a differential 'cost' effect. 10) Leakage: only Pre predictors were used, consistent with the schema's rule. Post items would be allowed only for an 'immediately after the workshop' framing, and this question does fit that framing for the outcome. 11) Only about 12 participants are lost at the merge, so merge skew is negligible. No demographic join was used, so the results cannot be stratified by Gender, Education or Native_State without a recomputed effective N.

Domain Expert Recommendations
  • Treat the finding as: 'Prior full-time intent is the dominant marker of Post full-time intent, and NGO / social-entrepreneurship-leaning preferences add a smaller, partly overlapping signal.' Avoid wording that implies Pre status alone explains most of the outcome beyond the correlated preferences.
  • Make the movers subgroup (is_fulltime_pre = 0, the schema's switched_to_fulltime) the primary analysis for programme design questions, and report overall metrics only as context. State explicitly that stayers inflate overall predictive power.
  • Fix one explicit rule for 'Not yet decided / confused' (null/NaN per the schema) and use it across all analyses. Report the resulting N (1,450 vs about 1,290) so that persistence and stayer-share figures are comparable.
  • Collapse the four overlapping rank items into one contrast index, for example (NGO + SE/NGO) minus (private + government) on the inverted scale. Alternatively use PCA or the schema's theme scores. This avoids the ipsative-overlap problem and gives a cleaner 'social vs conventional orientation' predictor.
  • Always control for or stratify by Workshop_Batch_Number, and flag batches 11–12 as small with ceiling stay rates. Consider ordinal modelling (Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement coded 1–5) to use the whole engagement ladder and not only the top-rung binary.
  • Extend to the schema's other candidate drivers (Life_Criteria_Cause_Of_Work, Life_Criteria_Financial_Security, Mindset_Visualizes_Self_As_Changemaker and Mindset_Social_Self_Efficacy_Confidence as Pre predictors, and Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global).   • To bridge intent and outcome, join to Demographics on string Participant_Id and relate Post intent to Current_Status_Of_Work and Person_Years_Of_Social_Action. Use the label_map, report the new effective N, and control for batch exposure years. This would show whether intent predicts realised social-impact careers.
  • For income, report medians or quantiles on the clipped (5,000–1,000,000), non-zero values with the effective N. Do not compare means, and do not interpret equal medians as no effect given heaping.
  • Report all results as associations from single-arm observational data, and avoid phrases such as 'the workshop leads participants to choose full-time'.

Theoretical Framework & Statistical Invariance

Persistence is nearly identical across runs: stay rate 0.92–0.93, mover rate 0.38–0.40, and 0.44 of Post full-timers already full-time at Pre (0.49 in Run 5's smaller sample). Bootstrap and Wilson CIs put the mover rate around 0.37–0.43. Mantel-Haenszel pooling across batches (OR 19.6 vs crude 18.3) shows the effect is not a batch artefact. All four preference directions hold in every run, under Cohen's d, Cliff's delta, trimmed d, batch-pooled d and top-2 shares. Full-sample effects are moderate to large for NGO and social-entrepreneurship preferences (d≈0.6–0.7) and smaller for private and government (|d|≈0.4). Among movers they roughly halve but stay significant. Movers-only AUC of about 0.63–0.65 confirms that stayers inflate overall predictive power. Expected income at 30 is not higher for full-timers: the median is 80k vs 100k overall and equal among movers.
