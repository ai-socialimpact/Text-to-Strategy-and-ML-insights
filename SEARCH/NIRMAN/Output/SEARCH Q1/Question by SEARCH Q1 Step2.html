Short answer

1. What Step 2 actually did

Step 2 was a screening step with two phases, and no predictive model yet.

  • Phase A built the cohort. It found 1,450 participants with both a Pre and a Post questionnaire, all at Workshop Level 0.1. It then cross-tabulated Pre full-time status against Post full-time status.

3. Why this happens (the mechanisms)

  1. Same question, same person, short gap. Pre and Post are the same survey item answered by the same person around one workshop. Stated career intentions rarely flip in that time, so most answers repeat.
  2. Commitment is sticky. Someone who already says "this is my full-time career" has little room to go up. They can only stay or drop, and only about 8% (28 of 365) dropped.
  3. People who arrive already committed tend to be the same people who rank NGO work highly. They are self-selected. Someone who signs up for a social-action workshop and already plans a full-time social career is likely to look similar on many Pre answers.
  4. A ceiling effect. For the Pre full-time group the starting point is already the top answer, so the Post answer mostly just reproduces it.

Analogy: if you ask people "Do you plan to be a runner?" before and after a running clinic, the best predictor of "yes" afterwards is "yes" beforehand. It tells you who was already a runner. It doesn't tell you what the clinic did.

  • It inflates apparent accuracy. A model that just says "Pre full-time → Post full-time" would be right for 92% of that group. The dossier says the 1,085 "not full-time at Pre" people are the more informative group.
