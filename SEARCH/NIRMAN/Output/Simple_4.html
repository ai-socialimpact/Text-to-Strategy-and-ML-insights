Domain Overview & Consensus Interpretation

The question profiles Batch 14 participants of the NIRMAN youth social-leadership programme who completed a Post-workshop psychometric survey, and then attaches their objective facts from the registry. The Questionnaire table (prod_nirman_questionnaire) has one row per (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase). Filtering to Batch 14 and Post therefore gives 205 response rows, which resolve to 179 distinct people. The 205 equals 148 + 22 + 35 (Workshop_Level 0.1 = Point 1, 0.2 = Point 2, 0.3 = Point 3). The 26 excess appearances come from participants who answered the Post survey at more than one Point. The runs describe this as 18 participants appearing at two or more levels. That is arithmetically consistent if about 10 participants appear at two levels and about 8 at all three. The Demographics table (prod_nirman_demographics) holds one row per Participant_Id. The one-to-many join therefore cannot inflate rows, and all 179 participants matched with 0 unmatched. This is plausible because Demographics covers batches 1–17 and the Questionnaire covers 7–17. The profile is roughly gender-balanced (91 MALE / 88 FEMALE) and 63.69% Student, with 114 Students on both Education_Status and Current_Status_Of_Work. It is Medical-heavy (99, 55.31%, where Medical excludes Pharmacy). Current_Age has mean 25.01 and median 24 (N = 179). Age_At_Workshop has mean 22.51 and median 22 (N = 178), because participant 14147 carries the 0 = unknown sentinel. The implied exposure (Current_Age − Age_At_Workshop) is about 2.5 years. Batch 14 is a relatively recent cohort, so its outcomes are partly censored. All five runs reproduce identical core figures. They differ only in add-on views (quartiles, level-weighted means, trimmed means, ranked and cumulative shares), which show the same data from different angles. This is single-arm observational data, so the results describe the cohort and prove nothing causal about the workshop.

5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Direct Distinct-Count & Arithmetic Mean Aggregation

This is the reference run and is the headline answer. Counting distinct Participant_Id respects the participant-level question rather than the response-row grain. That is why it gives 179 rather than 205, and why the per-level counts (148/22/35) exceed 179 in total. Defensive deduplication of Demographics is harmless because the schema guarantees one row per Participant_Id. A left join with a 179 + 0 = 179 reconciliation is the schema's prescribed way to report effective N. Age means are computed only on values > 0 (the unknown-age rule), which gives Current_Age N = 179 (mean 25.01, median 24) and Age_At_Workshop N = 178 (mean 22.51, median 22). 'Not Known' stays a real category in Education_Status and simply has count 0 in this cohort. The category shares come from the matched N of 179, so Current_Status_Of_Work is Student 114 (63.69%), Social Impact Work 37 (20.67%), Other Work 16 (8.94%), Educational Break 6 (3.35%), Different Trajectory 5 (2.79%), Previously SIW 1 (0.56%).

Run #2: Median-Centred Robust Distribution with IQR

The run deduplicates on (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level) before grouping. That mirrors the questionnaire grain and independently reproduces 148/22/35 and 179 distinct. It acts as a cross-check of Run 1's counts. For the ages it adds Q1/Q3/IQR: Current_Age is 23 / 24 / 27 (IQR 4, range 20–35). Age_At_Workshop is 20.25 / 22 / 24 (IQR 3.75, range 18–31). The values are consistent with a young cohort of roughly 20–28-year-olds, with a right tail of older participants. That tail pulls the means (25.01, 22.51) slightly above the medians (24, 22). The Age_At_Workshop floor of 18 and ceiling of 31 sit inside the schema range 0–32 once the 0 sentinel is removed. The run reports exclusions explicitly: 0 for Current_Age and 1 for Age_At_Workshop. This is the effective-N reporting the schema asks for. The 100.01% total for stream categories is rounding, not a data error.

Run #3: Weighted Estimation by Workshop_Level Sub-Sample Size

The volume-weighted means (25.06 and 22.62) differ from the pooled participant means (25.01 and 22.51) for a structural reason. Weighting by level sizes (148/22/35) gives a mean over (participant, level) rows with 205 and 204 valid ages. Participants who answered at several Points are therefore counted several times. They are probably the more engaged, later-stage cohort members. Level-wise means rise from Point 1 to Point 3 (Current_Age 24.40 → 25.41 → 27.66; Age_At_Workshop 22.26 → 23.14 → 23.80). This pattern is not a causal effect of level. Current_Age is an undated snapshot and Age_At_Workshop is age at the first (Point-1) workshop, so neither is age at the level shown. The pattern is consistent with Point 2/3 follow-up being invitation-gated and being reached over time. The weighted figure should be treated as a diagnostic of the overlap. It is not a better estimate of the cohort, which is why the consensus keeps the pooled participant-level values. The 100% match rate overall and within every level (148/148, 22/22, 35/35) confirms the join covers the whole ladder.

Run #4: 5% Trimmed Mean & Outlier-Filtered Age Statistics

Trimming is applied only to the age statistics, never to the categorical data or the row-level output, so counts and distributions are unchanged. With k = floor(0.05 × N) = 8 per tail, the trimmed N is 163 for Current_Age and 162 for Age_At_Workshop. Trimmed means are 24.87 and 22.38, which are 0.15 and 0.12 below the untrimmed values, and the medians stay at 24 and 22. Removed Current_Age values run from 20–21 (low) to 31–35 (high). Removed Age_At_Workshop values run from 18–19 (low) to 28–31 (high). These are legitimate ages, not data errors, since the schema range is 0–39 and 0–32. The small shift shows that the 0 sentinel was the only real data-quality issue, and it is already filtered out. The slightly lower trimmed mean reflects a mild right-skew from the few older participants, such as the 31–35 current-age group. It does not reflect errors.

Run #5: Rank-Order & Cumulative Share Distribution

Ranking confirms the dominant categories: MALE, Student (Education_Status), Medical (Educational_Stream_Category) and Student (Current_Status_Of_Work). Level shares use two different denominators and both are valid. Using 179 participants gives 82.68%, 12.29% and 19.55%, which sum above 100% because of multi-level overlap. Using 205 level rows gives 72.2%, 10.73% and 17.07%, which sum to 100%. Reading the level shares against the wrong denominator is the main way to misinterpret this table. The age bins show Current_Age concentrated in 22–24 (86, 48.04%) with a 28+ tail (42, 23.46%) and no one at 18 or under. Age_At_Workshop peaks at 19–21 (72, 40.45%) then 22–24 (61, 34.27%), with 4 at or under 18. This matches a cohort recruited in late college or early adulthood and surveyed about 2–3 years later. The percentile check passes: P50 equals the stated medians (24 and 22), and P25/P75 equal Run 2's quartiles (23/27 and 20.25/24).

Domain Nuances & Constraints

1) Grain: 205 is a count of response rows (participant × level) and 179 is a count of people. Any 'how many participants' statement must use 179. Per-level counts cannot be added to give the participant count. 2) Population definition: the cohort is defined by the Questionnaire's Workshop_Batch_Number and Post-phase rows, not by Demographics batch. Participants who completed only the Pre survey are excluded, so this is a Post-respondent subset (survey completion selection), not the full Batch 14 enrolment. The 100% match is expected because Demographics covers batches 1–17, but the runs did not check whether the Demographics batch equals 14 for every matched participant. 3) Age semantics: 0 = unknown, so 14147 is excluded from Age_At_Workshop only (N = 178). Age_At_Workshop is defined at the first Point-1 workshop, so it is not the age at Point 2/3. Current_Age is an undated snapshot. Level-wise age differences therefore reflect who progresses and when they are surveyed. 4) Outcome interpretation: Current_Status_Of_Work is an undated snapshot. 'Student' (114) is a censor-or-exclude class in the label map because the outcome is not yet realised. The 114 Students on both Education_Status and Current_Status_Of_Work look aligned, but the runs did not verify they are the same individuals. If they are, 37 of the 65 non-student participants (about 57%) are in Social Impact Work. That is a derived figure and was not computed in any run. Batch 14 is recent (17 is the latest), so outcomes are censored. Rates must not be compared with older cohorts without controlling for batch or exposure years. 5) Dominance of Medical: Educational_Stream_Category 'Medical' excludes Pharmacy, which sits in 'Arts/Commerce/Science/Pharmacy'. The matching detailed value in Educational_Stream is 'Medical / Public Health', and the two spellings must not be mixed. 6) Gaps in the consensus answer: Missing counts for Educational_Stream and Native_State, the Native_State distribution, and the Workshop_Name values were requested in Step 3 but are not reported in the consensus or run summaries. Workshop_Name is a low_coverage column in Demographics, so its null count is unknown here. Workshop_Name 'F' must be read as a string or it parses as boolean. 7) Weighted and trimmed statistics are sensitivity views. Pooled participant-level means and medians are the authoritative values. 8) This is single-arm observational data. Nothing here is causal evidence that the workshop produced any outcome, and all percentages are shares of matched N = 179. They may carry rounding drift (for example Educational_Stream_Category sums to 100.01%).
