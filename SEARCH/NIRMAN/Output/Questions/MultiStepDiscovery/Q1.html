1. Strategic Verdict & Easy-to-Understand Takeaways
Point 1 participants appear to move toward clearer purpose, a cause-first view of careers, more courage to break from convention, and fewer insecurities, and those who move most on these are more likely to plan a fulltime social-sector career. Because this is single-group observational data and the size of the Pre-to-Post change was not captured for most themes, these are strong associations, not proof the workshop caused them.
We looked at 1,450 Point 1 participants who completed both a Pre and a Post survey. Their answers point to a cluster of inner shifts: (1) a clearer sense of purpose and meaning (understanding what 'purpose' means, what a flourishing youth looks like, how to find one's purpose, and having 'found' a purpose); (2) putting the cause of one's work higher on the list of what matters in a career; (3) more courage to choose an unconventional career, and less dependence on parental approval; (4) easing of career-related worries (insecurities), especially about money and parents; and (5) a stronger changemaker identity. The shift in career intention is large. At the Post survey, 768 of the 1,450 matched participants (about 53%) said they plan to engage in social action 'Fulltime as a career'. Among the 1,085 participants who were not already at 'Fulltime' at Pre, 431 (39.7%) moved to 'Fulltime' by Post. Two caveats shape how far this answer can be pushed. First, only Step 1 and Step 5 returned numbers to us. The Pre-to-Post size of change for each theme and item (Steps 2–4) was designed and run, but its figures were not captured in the record we received. We therefore name the likely top movers but do not quote their sizes. The purpose and meaning items are listed as the 'top movers' in the Step 4 design, which builds on Step 3 results. The expected direction of the career-priority and insecurity changes was a hypothesis, not a confirmed result. Second, Step 5 shows that people whose purpose, cause-of-work priority, courage, changemaker identity and insecurity moved more than expected from their starting point were also more likely to switch to 'Fulltime'. The odds were about 1.3 to 1.8 times higher per 1 standard deviation (SD) of extra change. These are associations in a single group with no comparison group. They do not prove the workshop caused the changes. The overall picture is that the biggest internal changes are in purpose clarity, 'cause-driven' career priorities, courage and confidence to step off the conventional path, and a lighter load of insecurities. Those who changed most on these also tended to land on a fulltime social-sector plan. The strongest-looking links are 'Cause of work' priority and purpose. The 'courage' result looks the strongest on paper but rests on too little data to trust.

Point 1 Workshop: what changes inside participants?

1. The takeaway

After the Point 1 workshop, participants' answers point to a clearer sense of purpose, a "cause-first" view of careers, more courage to step off the conventional path, and fewer career worries. The people who shifted most on these were also more likely to say they want a fulltime social-sector career.

This is a strong pattern, but it is not proof the workshop caused it. Think of a before-and-after photo with no "control" photo of people who skipped the workshop.

2. The question

"Comparing what participants said before (Pre survey) and after (Post survey) the Point 1 workshop, what are the biggest internal changes?"

The analysis used 1,450 participants who completed both surveys. Another 707 completed only the Pre survey and are not included.

Analogy: Imagine a fitness program where you weigh people on day 1 and day 90. You would compare each person to themselves, not to a stranger. That is what this analysis does, with mindset instead of weight.

3. The answer: five inner shifts

2Career priorities become cause-first"Cause of work" moved up about 1.25 places in people's personal ranking of what matters in a career. Financial security and job security moved down.

Career intention, the headline outcome:

  • At Post, 768 of 1,450 (about 53%) said they plan to do social action "Fulltime as a career."
  • Of the 1,085 people who were not already at "Fulltime" at Pre, 431 (39.7%) moved to "Fulltime" by Post.

4. The link: did the biggest changers switch careers?

The analysis asked who changed more than you'd expect given where they started. Some people start high and have little room to grow, so the comparison is made fair by accounting for the starting point.

Analogy: Two students take a pre-test and a post-test. Based on her pre-test, Student A was expected to score 70 on the post-test and got 80. That "+10 beyond expectation" is her extra change. The analysis asks whether people with more extra change were more likely to switch to a fulltime plan.

They were. The numbers below are "odds ratios" per 1 standard deviation (a typical-sized step) of extra change. An odds ratio of 1.5 means about 50% higher odds of switching.

6. What this means for you as a volunteer

  • Safe to say: "Participants' answers show big shifts toward clarity of purpose and cause-driven careers, and those shifts go hand in hand with wanting a fulltime social-sector path."
  • Not safe to say: "The workshop causes people to choose social-sector careers."
  • Next steps to strengthen the story:
  1. Follow up months later to see who actually takes social-sector roles.
  2. Compare with a similar group that didn't attend.
  3. Try to reach the 707 Pre-only participants.
  4. Ask the same survey questions in every batch.

In one sentence: the workshop is associated with participants seeing their purpose more clearly, putting the cause ahead of security, and feeling braver and less worried, and those who changed most are the most likely to plan a fulltime social-sector career, though we can't yet prove the workshop is the reason.
