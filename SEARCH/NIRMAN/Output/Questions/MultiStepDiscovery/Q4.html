1. Strategic Verdict & Easy-to-Understand Takeaways
A participant's own Pre-workshop 'Fulltime as a career' answer is the dominant predictor of choosing full-time at their last workshop, and workshop level strongly confounds comparisons. Whether mindset and demographics add real predictive power is still unproven because the later modelling steps produced no reported results.
The clearest finding is that the best predictor of choosing 'Fulltime as a career' in the last workshop is what the participant already said before it began. Across 1,799 participants whose last workshop has a usable Post answer, about 59% (1,060 people) chose full-time. The rates were 52.1% at Level 0.1 (655 of 1,257), 68.0% at Level 0.2 (255 of 375) and 89.8% at Level 0.3 (150 of 167). In the Level 0.1 group, about 92% of people who already said 'Fulltime as a career' at Pre said it again at Post. That Pre answer is therefore a very strong signal, although it predicts stability and not change. The second pattern is that workshop level strongly shapes the answer. Full-time rates climb from 52% to 90% as participants move to later levels. People reach later workshops by being invited and by staying engaged, so those groups are self-selected. This is why every comparison was planned within a level. Simply comparing 'later-level people' with 'earlier-level people' would mostly measure who stayed in the programme. The Pre-answer rule is also incomplete. It catches only about half of the full-time choosers (roughly 337 of 655 at Level 0.1), because the other half were not full-time at Pre. For the volunteer team, the practical question is who converts. One important caveat: the numeric results for Steps 2 to 5 (screening, model accuracy, feature rankings, thresholds) were not reported. Only Step 1's numbers are confirmed outputs. The other figures above come from the experiment plans, which cite them as reference values to reproduce. We therefore cannot yet say whether mindset scores (purpose, changemaker identity, life priorities), career-preference ranks, income expectations or demographics add predictive power beyond the Pre answer. This dossier gives the confirmed answer and a clear plan to settle the open question. All findings describe association in observational data and do not show that the workshop caused any choice.

Plain-language guide: what predicts choosing "Full-time" at the last workshop

1. The question

If we look at a participant's last workshop, what tells us in advance whether they will choose "Fulltime as a career" as their level of engagement in social action?
  • "Last workshop" means the highest level a person reached (Level 0.1, 0.2 or 0.3).
  • The outcome is their answer to the career-plan question at that workshop. They either chose "Fulltime as a career" or something else. "Part time", "Volunteering frequently", "Occasionally", "Monetary contribution" and "Not yet decided / confused" all count as "not full-time".
  • "Predicts" means a signal available before the workshop, such as an earlier answer, mindset scores, priorities or demographics, that lines up with the outcome. It does not mean a cause.

2. The takeaway (one paragraph)

The best predictor is what the person already said before the workshop. People who said "Fulltime as a career" at the start almost always say it again at the end. Two cautions apply:

  1. Workshop level is tangled up with the result, because later levels contain more committed people.
  2. We have not yet proven that mindset scores, priorities or demographics add anything beyond the person's own earlier answer. The modelling steps that would test this produced no results.

3. The answer, step by step

Part A: How common is "Full-time" overall?

Among 1,799 participants whose last workshop has a usable "after" (Post) answer, about 59% (1,060 people) chose full-time.

Part B: The strongest predictor is the earlier answer

Analogy: Imagine asking diners what they plan to order before they open the menu, then checking what they actually order. Most people who said "the biryani" order the biryani. The earlier answer is a very good guess at the later one.

  • Among people who started as "Not yet decided / confused", about 49% still chose full-time. Early answers are useful but not fixed.

Part C: Why the earlier answer is not the whole story

Analogy: A metal detector that rarely beeps, but is almost always right when it does. It is very precise but misses a lot of treasure.
