4 4. Answer Contextualized by Schema & Domain Model
How the Project Schema Explains & Validates This Answer:

The question asks how many Batch 14 participants in NIRMAN, SEARCH's youth social-leadership program, say they plan to pursue social action 'Fulltime as a career' at the Pre and Post stage of the Point 1 workshop. It also asks how individuals move between 'full-time' and 'not full-time' across those two stages. This is subjective psychometric data, so it lives only in prod_nirman_questionnaire. Demographics holds objective facts, such as Current_Status_Of_Work, and is deliberately not joined. The questionnaire grain is one row per (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase). Filtering to Workshop_Batch_Number = 14 and Workshop_Level = 0.1 therefore yields at most one Pre row and one Post row per participant. All five runs found 296 rows: 148 Pre and 148 Post, with the same 148 people in both phases. The headline result is that 'Fulltime as a career' (ordinal value 5 of Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement) rose from 28/148 (18.92%) to 66/148 (44.59%). The matched transitions are 26 stayed full-time, 40 switched in, 2 switched out and 80 stayed not full-time. The net change is +38 and the switch-in rate is 40/120 = 33.33%. Because the data is single-arm observational with no comparison group, this is Pre→Post association and change, not evidence that the workshop caused it. The stated engagement outcome is a self-reported career plan, not an observed work status like Current_Status_Of_Work.

Schema Entity / Table Role in Answer Relevance & Meaning
prod_nirman_questionnaire Sole source table for the analysis. It is a Pre/Post psychometric survey with 4,304 rows, 2,177 participants and 181 columns, covering batches 7–17. Its grain, one row per (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase), explains why Pre and Post each have exactly one row per participant in this slice. The 'Fulltime as a career' response is the top of the 1–5 ordinal outcome scale and is the basis of the is_fulltime flag and the switched_to_fulltime recipe (restricted to pre == 0). Engagement has only 4 nulls in the whole table, and none fall in Batch 14 / Level 0.1, so no pairs were dropped. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Phase, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Batch_Number, Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement
Derived Pre/Post transition frame (pre_post_pairing recipe) An inner merge on Participant_Id within the same Workshop_Level, with _pre/_post suffixes. This is a merge, not a groupby, as the recipe prescribes. From it, is_fulltime_pre and is_fulltime_post are derived and cross-classified into Stayed full-time (1/1), Switched in (0/1), Switched out (1/0) and Stayed not full-time (0/0). The switch-in rate uses only the pre = 0 pool (120 people) as its denominator, matching the schema's switched_to_fulltime definition. The matched N of 148 equals the unpaired N in both phases, which is why the unpaired and matched net changes coincide. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement_pre, Career_Plan_Social_Action_Engagement_post, is_fulltime_pre, is_fulltime_post
prod_nirman_demographics Deliberately not used. It has 2,890 rows, one per Participant_Id, and covers batches 1–17. It would supply Current_Status_Of_Work, Gender, Age_At_Workshop and similar fields for follow-up or segmentation. Joining it would shrink and skew N, because not every participant appears in both tables. It would also bring in the objective outcome (actual work status), which is conceptually different from the self-declared plan measured here. The two tables share Participant_Id, Participant_Name, Workshop_Name and Workshop_Batch_Number, so any later join must cast the ID to string first. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Batch_Number, Current_Status_Of_Work, Gender, Age_At_Workshop
Domain Business Insights from Schema:
  • Present the headline as 'a stated full-time social-action career plan rose from 28 to 66 of 148 Batch 14 Point 1 participants (18.92% → 44.59%), driven by 40 switch-ins against 2 switch-outs', and always add 'association/change only, not causal'.
  • Quote the switch-in rate as 40/120 = 33.33% (conditional on pre = 0), and report the 26 stayed full-time and 2 switched out separately as retention (26/28 = 92.86%).
  • Report effective N at each step (4,304 → 296 → 148 per phase → 148 matched). State that matched N equals unpaired N in this batch because Pre and Post participant sets are identical, and re-verify this for other batches rather than assuming it.
  • To judge whether Batch 14 is typical, repeat the identical pipeline for batches 7–17 and show the per-batch net change and switch-in rate with Wilson or bootstrap intervals. Sort by Workshop_Batch_Number (17 = most recent).
  • Treat the Run 4 response-level breakdown (e.g. Volunteering frequently 52→16, Part time 51→65) as an interesting lead and verify it with a dedicated Pre×Post crosstab on the 1–5 ordinal before using it. It suggests much of the shift comes from people stepping up from lower engagement categories.
  • Complement this stated-plan analysis with the demographics outcome (Current_Status_Of_Work, Person_Years_Of_Social_Action) via a string-cast Participant_Id join to see whether Batch 14 switchers actually enter social-impact work. Recompute and report the effective N after the join, and control for censoring because Batch 14 is a recent cohort.
  • If modelling who switches in, use Pre-phase items and the theme scores plus demographics predictors (Gender, Age_At_Workshop, Educational_Stream_Category, Native_State). Restrict to pre = 0, and exclude Post items and deltas unless the question is explicitly about the immediate change. Expect a small sample (120 at-risk participants in this batch) and favour explainable models or pooled batches.
  • Communicate that the Wilson and bootstrap intervals show sampling stability within a single cohort. They do not remove confounding from time, self-selection or social-desirability in a Pre/Post design without a control group.
