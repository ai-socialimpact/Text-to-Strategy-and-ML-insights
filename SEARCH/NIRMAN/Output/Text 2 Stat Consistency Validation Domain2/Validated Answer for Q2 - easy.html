In-Depth Details & Synthesis:
Who was measured
1,307 people who answered the life-satisfaction question both before (Pre) and after (Post) the Point 1 workshop (Workshop Level 0.1). Each person is counted once.
  • Average score (1 = low, 5 = high): went from 2.90 before to 3.96 after, an average gain of about +1.06 points. The typical (median) person moved from 3 to 4, a gain of +1.
  • How individuals changed: 864 people (66.11%) scored higher, 311 (23.79%) stayed the same, and 132 (10.10%) scored lower.
  • Other workshop levels: Levels 0.2 and 0.3 (Points 2 and 3) have no matched before-and-after pairs (N = 0), so the 'all levels' total is the same as Level 0.1.
  • Is it just chance? No. Both statistical tests (paired t = 28.11, Wilcoxon z = 22.29) give p < 0.0001, meaning a change this large is very unlikely to be random noise.
  • Important caution: this is an observed change or association only. It does not prove the workshop caused the change.

4 4. Answer Contextualized by Schema & Domain Model
How the Project Schema Explains & Validates This Answer:

NIRMAN is SEARCH's youth social-leadership program. It records subjective psychometrics in the Questionnaire table (prod_nirman_questionnaire), captured Pre and Post each workshop, and objective facts in the Demographics table (prod_nirman_demographics). This question uses only the Questionnaire table. Its grain is one row per (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase): 4,304 rows, 2,177 participants, batches 7-17. The metric, Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global ('All things considered I am satisfied with my life'), is on the agreement_1_to_5 scale. It sits in the wellbeing_self theme and the headline_columns list, and the schema names a life-satisfaction gain of 2 or more as a secondary ML target. The consensus answer is that among 1,307 matched Point 1 (Workshop_Level 0.1) participants, mean life satisfaction rose from 2.90 (median 3) to 3.96 (median 4). The mean delta was +1.06 and the median delta +1. Improved / stayed the same / declined was 864 / 311 / 132 pairs (66.11% / 23.79% / 10.10%). The share agreeing (>= 4) rose from 32.06% to 72.07%. The data flow follows the schema recipe 'pre_post_pairing': 2,157 Pre rows and 2,147 Post rows were inner-merged on Participant_Id + Workshop_Level, giving 1,450 pairs. This matches the 1,450 matched Pre/Post Point-1 pairs cited in the schema's ML guidance. After the cleaning rule (0 = not answered, > 5 = out of range; 286 zeros and 1 out-of-range value) and dropping NaN pairs, 1,307 remained. Levels 0.2 and 0.3 produced no pairs, so the all-levels total is the Point 1 result restated. The schema defines this as single-arm observational data, so every finding is an association or observed change, never proof that the workshop caused it.

Only then can the bias from selective completion be judged. Report Pre-only dropout rate by Workshop_Batch_Number.

Report the effective N after the join, since the join shrinks and skews N. Use these cuts to see where the gain is largest, and remember that recent batches have had less time to realise outcomes.
  • Confirm directly with a Workshop_Level x Workshop_Phase count that Levels 0.2/0.3 have no Pre rows. If Point 2/3 surveys were collected under another coding or in another file, locate them before drawing conclusions about later-stage change. Until then, use Demographics' Point_2/Point_3 attended flags only as exposure descriptors.
  • Use the transition matrix, the agree/disagree shares and the median delta for program communication, because they respect the ordinal nature of the item. Treat the t-test and Wilcoxon p-values as confirmatory only.
  • If this metric is used as an ML target (the schema's 'Life-satisfaction gain >= 2', about 20.96% at +2 plus the +3/+4 tail), build predictors only from Pre-phase items and demographics. Never include Post-phase values or deltas as features, to avoid leakage. Report the base rate and use AUC/recall.
  • Do not describe the result as program effectiveness. If causal evidence is needed, consider follow-up Post measures at later points, a wait-list or comparison cohort, or linking the Pre-to-Post gain with the Demographics outcome (Current_Status_Of_Work) as an association only.
