4 4. Answer Contextualized by Schema & Domain Model
How the Project Schema Explains & Validates This Answer:

The question asks whether self-reported life satisfaction (Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global: 'All things considered I am satisfied with my life', agreement scale 1–5) changed from Pre to Post at the Point 1 workshop (Workshop_Level 0.1) for FEMALE participants whose Educational_Stream_Category is 'Medical' (Pharmacy excluded). In NIRMAN terms it combines objective facts about a person (Demographics: who they are, their gender and stream) with subjective psychometrics (Questionnaire: wellbeing captured Pre and Post each workshop). All five runs agree on the funnel and the result. The funnel is 749 female Medical participants → 627 present in the questionnaire → 619 with Point 1 rows → 368 Pre/Post pairs → 352 valid pairs. Mean life satisfaction rose from 2.86 to 3.84 (mean delta +0.98, median +1). Of the pairs, 216 (61.36%) improved, 91 (25.85%) stayed the same and 45 (12.78%) declined. The paired t-test (t(351)=12.73), Wilcoxon (z=10.48) and sign test all show a strong upward shift. Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global is part of the wellbeing_self theme and is a named secondary ML target ('life-satisfaction gain ≥ 2'). This is single-arm observational data, so the result describes association and change, not proof that the workshop caused it. It also describes only completers who answered both Pre and Post. The funnel shows the Post response is the main bottleneck (619 Pre vs 368 Post rows).

Schema Entity / Table Role in Answer Relevance & Meaning
prod_nirman_demographics Defines the cohort: one row per unique Participant_Id (2,890 rows). It supplies the Gender and Educational_Stream_Category filters that produce N1 = 749. Both columns are in the zero-missing 'usable' tier, so the filter itself does not lose rows to nulls. 'Medical' in Educational_Stream_Category excludes Pharmacy, and the detailed Educational_Stream value 'Medical / Public Health' must not be mixed in. The table covers batches 1–17, wider than the questionnaire, which is why only a subset of these participants can be paired. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Gender, Educational_Stream_Category, Workshop_Batch_Number, Workshop_Name
prod_nirman_questionnaire Holds the outcome. The grain is one row per (Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase), so a participant can have at most one Pre and one Post row at Level 0.1, which is why the runs found 0 duplicates. Restricting to Level 0.1 gives 987 rows covering 619 participants. The Pre rows (619) outnumber the Post rows (368), so the Pre/Post inner merge, not the demographic filter, drives most of the attrition. The questionnaire covers batches 7–17 only, but the paired data appear only in batches 11–17. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Level, Workshop_Phase, Workshop_Batch_Number, Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global
Join (Participant_Id, one-to-many) Participant_Id must be cast to string in both frames before any join, groupby or comparison, because it looks numeric (1001–17176) but is a key. Demographics is one row per participant and the questionnaire has many rows, so the join shrinks and skews N. That is why the stage counts N1, N2, N2b and N3 must be reported. The tables share the column names Participant_Name, Workshop_Name and Workshop_Batch_Number. Run 5 avoided collisions by keeping only Participant_Id from demographics and stratifying on the questionnaire batch (Workshop_Batch_Number_pre). Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Name, Workshop_Batch_Number, Participant_Name
Cleaning and scale rules (agreement_1_to_5) Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global is in the out_of_range_to_nan list for the agreement scale. A value of 0 means 'not answered', not 'very low satisfaction', and values above 5 are invalid. The runs report 32 zero values set to NaN in the Level 0.1 frame. Leaving those zeros in would have artificially lowered Pre means and inflated deltas. The 16 pairs lost between the 368 merged pairs and the 352 valid pairs are consistent with this cleaning. The other zeroed values presumably sit in unpaired rows, which was not verified. Relevant Columns: Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global, Workshop_Phase, Workshop_Level
Domain Business Insights from Schema:
  • Present the final answer as a labelled funnel with consistent names: N1=749 (demographics), 627 participants in the questionnaire, 619 at Point 1, 368 merged Pre/Post pairs, and 352 valid pairs. Use participants for counts and rows only where explicitly labelled.
  • Describe the result as 'among completers with valid Pre and Post scores'. Quantify attrition by comparing Pre scores and batches of the 251 Pre-only participants with those of the 352 pairs, to show whether non-response is selective.
  • Confirm with the programme team whether Post responses were collected only from batch 11 onward. Document that batches 7–10 are Pre-only, so the pairing limit is a data-collection fact, not a cleaning error.
  • Report the gain as an ordinal result alongside the mean: median +1, 61.36% improved, 12.78% declined, 35.5% gaining ≥2 points. Use Wilcoxon or the transition matrix as the primary test and the t-test as a consistency check.
  • Extend the same pipeline to male and non-Medical participants for context. Medical women alone cannot show whether this gain is distinctive. Keep the matching rules identical (string Participant_Id, Level 0.1, 0→NaN, 1–5 validity).
  • Treat small batches (11, 17) cautiously and consider Batch 15's weight. Report batch-stratified results next to pooled results, and note that heterogeneity is borderline (p≈0.05–0.08).
  • If this outcome is used in ML, follow the schema's leakage rules. Pre-phase items and demographics may predict the delta, but Post items are allowed only for immediate-change questions. Control for Workshop_Batch_Number and report effective N after the merge and dropNa.
  • Keep the caveat 'association, not causation' in every output, and mention that no comparison group exists and that Point 2/3 follow-up is invitation-gated, so it is not a randomised exposure.
