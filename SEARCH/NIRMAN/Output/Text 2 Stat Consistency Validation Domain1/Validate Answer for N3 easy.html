4 4. Answer Contextualized by Schema & Domain Model
How the Project Schema Explains & Validates This Answer:

The question asks for a Pre-workshop life-satisfaction baseline for one narrowly defined NIRMAN cohort: Batch 14, FEMALE, Educational_Stream_Category = 'Medical' (which excludes Pharmacy). It uses both tables. Demographics (prod_nirman_demographics, one row per Participant_Id, 2,890 rows) holds the objective facts used to define the cohort. Questionnaire (prod_nirman_questionnaire, one row per Participant_Id × Workshop_Level × Workshop_Phase, 4,304 rows, batches 7–17 only) holds the subjective psychometric item Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global ('All things considered I am satisfied with my life'). That item sits in the wellbeing_self theme on the agreement_1_to_5 scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree, agree = 4 or 5). All five runs agree on the following. The cohort is 52 participants. Each has exactly one Pre / Point-1 (Workshop_Level 0.1) row, so there are 0 duplicates and 0 drops. One participant (ID 14045) answered 0, which the schema defines as 'not answered' rather than a low score, so valid N = 51. Among the valid respondents the mean is 3.02, the median is 3, and the frequencies for scores 1–5 are 3/18/11/13/6. Agreement is 19/51 = 37.25% of valid respondents, or 19/52 = 36.54% of the cohort. In domain terms, this is a descriptive, pre-intervention snapshot of how satisfied young women medical-stream participants in Batch 14 were with their lives as they began the Point-1 workshop. Responses cluster around the neutral midpoint with slightly more disagreement (21) than agreement (19). It is single-arm observational data, so it supports no claim about workshop effect.

Schema Entity / Table Role in Answer Relevance & Meaning

Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global is on the 1–5 agreement scale. It is in the out_of_range_to_nan list (>5 becomes NaN), and 0 means 'not answered' per the cleaning rules. The table covers batches 7–17 only, and Batch 14 falls inside that range. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id, Workshop_Phase, Workshop_Level, Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global, Workshop_Batch_Number
Join relationship (Participant_Id, one-to-many) Demographics (1 row) maps to Questionnaire (many rows). The question requires a semi-join (cohortSet.has(id) row filter) rather than a merge, so that the cohort is never multiplied across Pre/Post and Level 0.1/0.2/0.3 rows. Participant_Id must be cast to a trimmed string in both tables because it is a key, not a number. Effective N must be reported after the join: here it is 52 of 52 with a Pre row, so there is no join shrinkage. That is a property of this cohort, not a general guarantee, because not every participant appears in both tables. Relevant Columns: Participant_Id
Data-quality and scale rules (cleaning and scales blocks) These rules explain why ID 14045 is excluded: 0 is 'not answered' (set to NaN), not a very low satisfaction score. They also explain the exclusive bucket order and the 'agree = score ≥ 4' definition. The fact that Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global is not on the reverse-coded list means the raw 1–5 values can be interpreted directly, with higher meaning more satisfied. Relevant Columns: Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global
Domain Business Insights from Schema:
  • Present the headline satisfaction figure as 37.25% agreement among 51 valid respondents, with the 36.54% cohort-based figure labelled as context only. State the effective N (51 of 52) and why ID 14045 is excluded (0 = not answered).
  • Report distribution shape alongside the mean. The median of 3, the modal score of 2 and the 21 / 11 / 19 split across disagree, neutral and agree convey the neutral-to-slightly-dissatisfied baseline better than 3.02 alone, because the scale is ordinal.
  • Keep the cohort definition strictly on Educational_Stream_Category = 'Medical' (excluding Pharmacy). If a broader 'health' cohort is wanted, define it explicitly rather than mixing it with Educational_Stream 'Medical / Public Health'.
  • Keep the semi-join approach and the explicit effective-N reporting when extending this to other batches or streams. Batches 1–6 will have no questionnaire data, and participants may be present in one table and absent from the other.
  • To study change, extend this baseline with the Pre/Post inner merge on matched Point-1 pairs (Workshop_Level 0.1) and a paired test on the delta. Keep the claims associational, given the single-arm design and the invitation-gated Point 2/3 exposure.
  • Compare this cohort to other Batch 14 groups (for example, male participants or non-Medical streams) with an unpaired test and an effect size. The sample is small, so the Wilson intervals indicate that large differences would be needed before drawing conclusions.
  • Retain the zero-duplicate audit and the bucket reconciliation as standing data-quality checks, since they cheaply confirm that the questionnaire's grain and the cleaning rules are being honoured.
  • Fix the template note in the robustness run that says trimming 'may remove nothing (k = 0)' when k is computed as 2. It is harmless to the results but could mislead a reader.
