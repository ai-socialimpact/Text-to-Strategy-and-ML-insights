4 4. Answer Contextualized by Schema & Domain Model
How the Project Schema Explains & Validates This Answer:

The question asks whether, in one NIRMAN sub-cohort, self-reported life satisfaction rose from the Pre to the Post survey at the first (Point-1) workshop. The cohort is Batch 14, FEMALE participants whose Educational_Stream_Category is 'Medical' (Pharmacy excluded). Demographics (objective facts) defines the cohort. The Questionnaire (subjective psychometrics) supplies the outcome, Metric_Life_Satisfaction_Global, an agreement_1_to_5 item in the wellbeing_self theme (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree; agree >= 4). The cohort funnel is 148 Batch-14 rows, then 81 female, then 52 'Medical', which matches the expected N = 52. All 52 have exactly one Pre and one Post Point-1 row, so there is no attrition and no duplication. One Pre answer is 0, which the schema defines as 'not answered', so it is excluded. That leaves valid N of 51 Pre, 52 Post and 51 pairs. Mean life satisfaction moved from 3.02 to 3.96, and the share agreeing (score >= 4) moved from 37.25% to 69.23%. Among the 51 pairs the mean delta is +0.92 (median +1). 33 participants improved, 13 stayed the same and 5 declined. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 38 non-zero deltas gives W+ = 657, W− = 84 and |z| = 4.25. This is single-arm observational data, so it shows association and change, not that the workshop caused the change.

Final Answer: Life Satisfaction 'Agree' Rate Rose After the Workshop (Pre vs Post, Batch 14 Female Medical Cohort)

Final conclusion at a glance. Of the 52 participants, the share who agree (score 4 or 5) that they are satisfied with life jumped from 37.25% before the workshop to 69.23% after. Average scores rose from 3.02 to 3.96 (paired: 3.02 to 3.94), a mean gain of +0.92 points per person. This is the headline result that all 5 independent methods reproduced. The pattern is descriptive only; it shows association, not proof that the workshop caused the change.
