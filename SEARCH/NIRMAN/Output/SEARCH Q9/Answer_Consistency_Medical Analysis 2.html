This run puts every attribute on one standardized scale. Categorical levels are one-hot encoded and given binary SMDs, and numeric attributes get pooled-SD SMDs, as in propensity-balance diagnostics. Domain Overview & Consensus Interpretation

The question asks whether a '338-person cohort' of NIRMAN participants (SEARCH's youth social-leadership program) can be reproduced from the Demographics table, and whether a Medical background is the biggest difference between 'Early Leavers' and 'Sustainers'. Both groups come from the primary outcome column Current_Status_Of_Work. Early Leavers are 'Previously SIW', the YAML label 'engaged_then_left'. Sustainers are 'Social Impact Work', the YAML label 'positive'. Medical is Educational_Stream_Category == 'Medical', which excludes Pharmacy. The table has 2,890 rows with unique Participant_Id values, so counting unique IDs and counting rows give the same result and there are no duplicates. Under these literal definitions the cohort is 572 people: 99 Early Leavers, 473 Sustainers and 40 Medical Early Leavers (40.4%). None of the expected 338/142/196/120 (84.5%) is reproduced. All five runs agree on this, so the discrepancy is not a method artefact. The expected figures most likely come from a different extract, a different outcome-label mapping, or a different filter, because the schema contains no combination of Current_Status_Of_Work labels that yields them. The observed Medical association is modest: 40.4% vs 28.75% (+11.65 pp), crude OR about 1.68 (CI 1.07–2.63), chi-square 5.22, p about 0.02. Workshop_Batch_Number is the largest difference (Cohen's d about -0.59, r about 0.28). Early Leavers sit in earlier batches (mean about 6.1 vs 8.8). That pattern fits the schema's censoring rule: recent batches have had less time to realise outcomes. Controlling for batch weakens the Medical association (MH OR 1.40–1.46, adjusted logistic OR 1.39–1.61, all CIs include 1). Both claims are therefore Not confirmed. This is single-arm observational data, so only association can be reported, not causation.

5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Classical Contingency-Table Baseline (Pearson Chi-Square, Cramér's V, Wald Odds Ratio, Cohen's d)

This run is the baseline. It confirmed 2,890 rows = 2,890 unique Participant_Id values, so there are no duplicates. It then counted 99 'Previously SIW' and 473 'Social Impact Work'. The Medical 2x2 table is 40/99 vs 136/473, giving a Woolf OR of 1.68 (1.07–2.63), chi-square 5.22 and phi/V of about 0.10 (a small effect). The ranking used V for categorical attributes and d converted to r for numeric ones. Workshop_Batch_Number comes first (d = -0.59, r = 0.28; mean batch 6.13 vs 8.78). That fits the schema: the early Demographics-only batches (1–6) contain many people who have had years to either sustain or leave, while batches 13–17 are mostly still Students or unresolved. Age, Workshops_Attended_Count and the 4-level stream variable follow. Medical ranks 4th (5th when the parent stream attribute is listed). Education_Status could not be scored because every cohort member is 'Graduate'. That makes sense: people with outcomes such as Social Impact Work or Previously SIW are graduates by construction, and the 'Student' count of 1,493 matches the work-status 'Student' count. The run's batch-band Mantel-Haenszel OR of 1.46 (0.92–2.31) shows that the Medical effect partly reflects batch mix.

Run #2: Standardized Mean Difference (Covariate-Balance SMD) Ranking

This run puts binary and numeric attributes on one SMD scale, as in covariate-balance diagnostics. Medical's SMD is 0.25, which is above the 0.1 imbalance threshold but well below the 0.5 'large' threshold. Batch is -0.66 (large) and Age is -0.28, using the 62 + 326 participants with Age_At_Workshop > 0. Workshops_Attended_Count is 0.22, Gender 0.05 and Education_Status 0.00. Medical therefore ranks 3rd, higher than in the other runs, because Age and dosage are scaled differently under SMD than under V or Cliff's delta. SMD is also sensitive to the lower-variance binary Medical indicator. The Education_Status SMD of 0 reflects no variation (every member is 'Graduate'), not balance. 'Student' and 'Not Known' are undefined. The run's Mahalanobis D (0.90 with age, 0.66 without) shows the groups differ mainly through cohort timing, not through stream. Its check of the user-specified 42% base rate (142/338) against the observed 22.73% share of Medical people who are Early Leavers shows the expected numbers belong to a different population. Against the observed base rate of 17.31%, the lift is 1.31 and not significant.

Run #3: Robust Non-Parametric Testing (Fisher Exact, Bias-Corrected Cramér's V, Mann-Whitney U / Cliff's Delta, Median & IQR)

Batch number (1–17), dosage (1–4) and age are bounded, discrete and tied, so rank-based tests suit them. Fisher p = 0.03 with a conditional MLE OR of 1.68 (exact CI 1.04–2.69) shows the Medical result does not depend on large-sample approximations. Bergsma bias-corrected V (0.09 for Medical) removes the small-cell inflation of Cramér's V. Batch has a Cliff's delta of -0.31 (median batch 5 for Early Leavers vs 8 for Sustainers), Age -0.13 (N = 388) and dosage 0.12 (p = 0.04). Medical ranks 4th of 10. The scales (V vs Cliff's delta) are only loosely commensurable, and Medical (0.09) sits close to Age (0.13) and dosage (0.12). The rank is therefore fragile, but its distance from Batch is not. Wilson and Newcombe CIs show how wide the uncertainty is (gap 1.6 to 22.2 pp). Education_Status has only one level in this cohort, so its corrected V is about 0.

Run #4: Seeded Bootstrap Resampling of Effect Sizes and Rank Stability

This run tests whether the ranking is stable. Batch is #1 in 99% of resamples (|r| = 0.28, CI 0.21–0.36). Medical is #1 in 0% (median rank 5, V = 0.10, CI 0.01–0.18). The Medical OR bootstrap CI is 1.07–2.56 and the pp-gap CI is 1.46–21.68, so Medical is positively associated but its lower bound is close to no effect. The run's plan assumed group sizes of 142 and 196, but the data gave 99 and 473, so those sizes were an inherited expectation. The reported CI-overlap flag (Medical 0.01–0.18 vs Batch 0.21–0.36) looks wrong, but the verdict is unaffected because Medical fails the #1 criterion. Education_Status shows V = 0 with CI 0–0, which is degenerate because it has no variation. Age uses 62 + 326 = 388 after the 0 = unknown filter. The consistent Batch dominance reflects censoring and cohort age structure more than a stable property of participants.

Run #5: Multivariable Logistic Regression with Likelihood-Ratio Deviance Ranking and Batch-Stratified Mantel-Haenszel OR

This run handles confounding by batch, as the schema's censoring rule demands ('control for batch or exposure years'). The MH OR is 1.40 (0.88–2.23), with batches banded into 14 strata (only 13–14 and 15–17 merged). Breslow-Day (p = 0.0014) is unreliable because many strata have sparse or zero cells (the 13–17 band has about 3 Early Leavers). In the main model (Age_At_Workshop > 0, N = 388, 184 dropped) the Medical OR is 1.61 (0.77–3.37). Without age (N = 572) it is 1.39 (0.80–2.41). Neither is significant. Workshop_Batch_Number is #1 by LR chi-square (25.63 and 26.52), with about 0.36 OR per SD (later batches have fewer Early Leavers). The stream block is #2 (chi-square 5.77, p = 0.12). Education_Status dummies were dropped for lack of variance. The model has AUC 0.74 and McFadden R² 0.12, against 0.60 and 0.03 for Medical alone. The Medical-only OR on the age-known subset (2.41) differs from the crude 1.68, which suggests people with unknown age (0) differ systematically. That is a known data quality trait of the age columns.

Domain Nuances & Constraints

1) Count mismatch. The YAML defines the labels but not the 338/142/196/120 counts. Under the literal definitions, the figures are 572/99/473/40. The 84.5% Medical share is not supported. The expected figures may come from a different data extract, additional filters (for example restricting to certain batches or a cohort of experienced participants), or a different label assignment (for example including 'Educational Break' as leaning-positive). Strict, normalised and fuzzy matching, and simple batch or gender filters, all failed to reproduce them. 2) Outcome definition. Current_Status_Of_Work is an undated snapshot, so 'Early Leaver' means 'engaged and later left' with no transition dates. Time to leave cannot be modelled. The label_map treats 'Previously SIW' as a distinct engaged_then_left class. 3) Censoring. Recent batches (higher numbers) have had less time to realise outcomes. Batch is therefore the dominant discriminator, and the Medical association shrinks and loses significance after control. The 13–17 band is very sparse (about 3 Early Leavers), so those estimates are unstable. 4) Data quality. Age_At_Workshop 0 = unknown. Filtering to > 0 reduces N to 388 (62 EL, 326 S), a non-random subset, as the changed Medical-only OR on that subset suggests. Education_Status has only 'Graduate' in this cohort, so 'Not Known' never appears and the attribute carries no information. It also appears derived from work status (the 'Student' count equals the work-status 'Student' count), so it is a by-product of the group definition. 5) Metric comparability. Cramér's V, Cohen's d, Cliff's delta and SMD are not on the same scale, which is why Medical ranks 3rd to 5th. Batch is first in every method. Mixing the 4-level stream attribute with its Medical indicator double counts the same information. 6) Medical spelling. 'Medical' (Category, excludes Pharmacy) differs from 'Medical / Public Health' (Educational_Stream). The Category value was used correctly. 7) Single-arm observational data with multiple testing and no causal design. Medical over-representation among Early Leavers could reflect career-pathway constraints or demand outside the data, and the data cannot test that. Run-specific slips (Run 4's assumed group sizes of 142/196 and misreported CI overlap; Run 5's Breslow-Day on sparse strata) do not change any verdict.

The five methods differ in effect-size metric: Cramér's V with Cohen's d, SMD, bias-corrected V with Cliff's delta, bootstrap, and logistic LR chi-square. They still reach the same conclusions.

  • Rank: Medical ranks 3rd (SMD) to 5th depending on the metric, and counting the parent stream attribute moves it by one place. Batch is #1 in every run (r about 0.28, d about -0.59, Cliff's delta -0.31, SMD -0.66; #1 in 99% of bootstrap resamples; top LR attribute).   • Data caveats: Education_Status has only the value 'Graduate' within the cohort, so it carries no information and 'Not Known' never appears.
  • Run-specific slips: Run 4's bootstrap prompt assumed group sizes of 142 and 196, but the data gave 99 and 473. Run 4 also misreports CI overlap. Run 5's Breslow-Day test is unreliable with sparse strata.
