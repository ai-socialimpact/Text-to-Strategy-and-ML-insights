Domain Overview & Consensus Interpretation

The claim is a descriptive comparison of educational background and work outcome within the NIRMAN youth social-leadership programme run by SEARCH. It uses only the Demographics table (prod_nirman_demographics), which holds objective facts about each participant. That table has one row per unique Participant_Id (2,890 rows). The claim needs three zero-missing, 'usable' columns: Participant_Id, Current_Status_Of_Work (the primary outcome, an undated snapshot of present work status) and Educational_Stream_Category (a roll-up in which 'Medical' explicitly excludes Pharmacy). All five runs defined 'Sustainers' as Current_Status_Of_Work = 'Social Impact Work' (473 participants). All other statuses were 'Non-Sustainers' (2,417). Medical share is 136/473 (28.75%) among Sustainers and 1,209/2,417 (50.02%) among Non-Sustainers, a gap of about -21.27 pp (ratio 0.57, OR about 0.40). No join to the questionnaire is needed. The effective N is therefore the full 2,890, with no low-coverage filter, no nulls and no duplicates. In domain terms, Sustainers are drawn more evenly from Arts/Commerce/Science/Pharmacy (33.62%), Medical (28.75%), Engineering (20.93%) and Other (16.70%). The Non-Sustainer pool is dominated by Medical participants (50.02%). That is largely because 1,493 Non-Sustainers are still 'Student' and 880 of them (58.94%) are Medical. Medical students are heavily represented in the not-yet-realised outcome pool, so the headline gap partly reflects where people are in their journey. It is not a pure statement about who ends up in social-impact work. The direction is robust across all methods. The size and significance depend on how the censored statuses ('Student', 'Not Sure') are handled. The data is single-arm observational, so this is an association and never a causal claim.

5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Direct Prevalence (Proportion) Comparison

This run sets the baseline. It confirms 2,890 rows before and after de-duplication on Participant_Id, with no nulls in the two substantive columns. It reports Medical shares of 28.75% (136/473) for Sustainers, 50.02% (1,209/2,417) for Non-Sustainers and 46.54% (1,345/2,890) overall. That is a gap of -21.27 pp with a ratio of 0.57. The stream-by-flag table shows the structural reason. Sustainers are spread across Arts/Commerce/Science/Pharmacy (159, 33.62%), Medical (136), Engineering (99, 20.93%) and Other (79, 16.70%). Non-Sustainers are half Medical, and also over-represent Arts/Commerce/Science/Pharmacy (613). Medical is the largest stream among Non-Sustainers but only second among Sustainers. The run states that 'Student' (1,493) and 'Other Work' (541) dominate the comparison group. That already hints that the comparison pool is shaped by programme timing and not only by stream.

Run #2: 2x2 Contingency Chi-Square / Two-Proportion Z-Test with Wilson CIs

This run tests whether the -21 pp gap is distinguishable from sampling noise. The 2x2 table is 136 Medical and 337 non-Medical among Sustainers, and 1,209 and 1,208 among Non-Sustainers. There are no zero cells, so no 0.5 correction was needed. The Wilson intervals (0.25–0.33 vs 0.48–0.52) do not overlap, and z = -8.48 with chi-square 71.92 (z²) gives p well below 0.01. OR is 0.40 [0.33, 0.50] and RR is 0.57 [0.50, 0.67]. With 2,890 unique participants the test is highly powered, so the statistical significance is unsurprising. What it does not address is composition. The test treats 'Non-Sustainer' as one homogeneous population, although it is 62% 'Student' (1,493 of 2,417) and includes 'Previously SIW', 'Educational Break' and 'Not Sure'. 'Statistically significant' therefore means the pooled difference is real in this registry. It does not mean Sustainers differ from every alternative outcome group.

Run #3: Rank-Order Share Across All Work-Status Groups (incl. Sustainers vs Previously SIW)

This run unpacks the pooled comparison group using all seven Current_Status_Of_Work values. Sustainers rank 3rd lowest of 7 in Medical share (28.75%), behind Other Work (19.96%) and Different Trajectory (21.95%). They sit below Previously SIW (40.40%, n=99, an 11.65 pp gap, the schema-designated sustained-vs-dropout contrast) and below the pooled non-Sustainers. The pooled Non-Sustainer share is driven by two groups. 'Student' (n=1,493, 58.94% Medical) supplies 65.43% of all Medical participants. 'Educational Break' (n=167) is 88.62% Medical, which is unusually high and worth domain follow-up. One plausible explanation, not proven in the data, is that medical trainees take internship, entrance-exam or study breaks. Student plus Educational Break account for 76.43% of all Medical participants, and Sustainers contribute 10.11%. After removing the censored statuses 'Student' and 'Not Sure' (effective N = 1,362, 450 Medical), the pooled Non-Sustainer share falls to 35.32% (314/889). The gap shrinks to 6.57 pp, and Sustainers still rank 3rd of 5. Descriptive z-tests keep the direction (p = 0.022 vs Previously SIW, p = 0.014 vs the uncensored pool), but the margin is much smaller. The lesson is that the headline gap is real but mostly reflects a Medical-heavy Student pool whose outcome is not yet realised. The claim holds in direction against every comparator that has realised an outcome, but Sustainers are not the least-Medical group. The run did not control Workshop_Batch_Number and the Previously SIW group is small, so its shares are unstable.

Run #4: Bootstrap Confidence Interval & Permutation Test of the Share Difference

This run drops normal-approximation assumptions. It runs 2,000 stratified bootstrap resamples (mulberry32, seed 42, group sizes fixed at 473 and 2,417) and 2,000 label permutations. Observed D is -0.21. The bootstrap 95% CI is [-0.26, -0.17], so the upper bound is below 0 (the strongest decision tier). D < 0 occurs in 100% of resamples, and the permutation p is below 1/2000. The result matches Runs 1 and 2 because both groups are large, so parametric and resampling methods converge. It confirms that the pooled -21 pp gap is not a small-sample artefact. Like Run 2, it resamples participants as if they were exchangeable. It cannot correct for the mix of statuses, batch timing or censoring in the Non-Sustainer pool. It gives precision on the pooled difference, not robustness to the definition of the comparison group.

Run #5: Batch-Stratified Mantel-Haenszel Odds Ratio & Logistic Regression

This run addresses censoring and cohort effects directly. Workshop_Batch_Number is the YAML's time axis, and recent cohorts have had less time to reach 'Social Impact Work'. The MH pooled OR is 0.39 [0.32, 0.49]. The adjusted logistic OR (Sustainer, standardised batch, gender one-hot, L2 = 0.01) is 0.58 [0.49, 0.68]. The CI is approximate because of the penalty. The two differ for two reasons. Batch enters the regression only as a linear term but has a non-linear relationship with the outcome. The L2 penalty also shrinks the coefficient toward 0. The strata show heterogeneity. Batches 1–6 show no effect (OR 0.97), while batches 7–10, 11–13 and 14–17 show ORs of 0.21, 0.08 and 0.35. This fits the domain story that older cohorts had time to mature, so Student status is rare there. Medical participants in recent batches are disproportionately still Students or on Educational Break. The sensitivity run excludes 'Student' and 'Not Sure' (N = 1,362; strata 631 / 367 / 137 / 227). It removes 1,528 rows, almost all non-Sustainers, and 921 of them come from batches 14–17. This is the clearest evidence of censoring in the data. The MH OR rises to 0.81 [0.63, 1.04] and the adjusted OR to 0.84 [0.68, 1.03]. Both CIs include 1. Strata ORs in batches 1–6 and 14–17 flip above 1 (1.24 and 1.15), while 7–10 and 11–13 remain below 1 (0.45 and 0.35). Direction is preserved in the pooled estimates, but significance is lost. The strong association depends heavily on how Student and Not Sure are classified.

Domain Nuances & Constraints

1) Single-arm observational registry data: the result is an association between stream and current work status. It is not evidence that a medical background causes or prevents sustained social action, and it says nothing about the workshop's effect. 2) 'Sustainers' is an analyst's operationalisation: Current_Status_Of_Work = 'Social Impact Work' only. It is an undated snapshot. The schema's other 'sustained' lens, Person_Years_Of_Social_Action (3+ years), is low coverage (majority null), so it was not used. 3) The 'Non-Sustainer' bucket is heterogeneous: 1,493 Students (62%), 541 Other Work, 167 Educational Break, 99 Previously SIW, 82 Different Trajectory and 35 Not Sure. The YAML label_map says Student and Not Sure should be censored or excluded because the outcome is not yet realised. Including them is what drives the large -21 pp gap and the strong test statistics. Excluding them shrinks the gap to about 6.6 pp, with non-significant adjusted estimates. 4) Censoring and cohort heterogeneity: recent batches have had less time to realise outcomes. Batches 1–6 show no Medical deficit (OR 0.97), while later strata show strong deficits, so the pooled MH OR hides heterogeneity. 5) Educational Break is 88.62% Medical. The reason is not in the schema and should be treated as a hypothesis, for example internship or exam-preparation breaks. 6) Definition traps: Medical here is Educational_Stream_Category == 'Medical', which excludes Pharmacy. Educational_Stream ('Medical / Public Health') would give different counts and must not be mixed in. 7) Data-quality status: all columns used are zero-missing, so the effective N is 2,890 in the main analyses and 1,362 in the sensitivity analyses. No join was made, so there is no join-induced shrinkage. Batches 1–6 exist only in Demographics, so any extension to questionnaire variables would drop them. 8) Statistical significance at N = 2,890 is easy to reach. Practical interpretation should rely on the effect sizes and the censoring-sensitivity results, not on p-values alone.

Domain Expert Recommendations
  • State the claim with its definition: 'Among participants currently in Social Impact Work, the Medical-stream share (Pharmacy excluded) is 28.75%, versus 50.02% among all other statuses combined.' Include the sensitivity result (about 35% once Student and Not Sure are removed) so readers do not over-read the 21 pp gap.
  • Treat the censor-excluded specification (Student and Not Sure removed, N = 1,362) as the primary analysis, in line with the YAML label_map. Report the pooled all-status result as a secondary view. The direction is robust, but only a small and statistically uncertain effect survives censoring.
  • Use Previously SIW as the sustained-vs-dropout contrast (Sustainers 28.75% vs 40.40%, n=99). Flag its small n. Also consider a Person_Years_Of_Social_Action ≥ 3 definition of 'sustained', reporting the shrunken effective N and never imputing nulls as 0.
  • Investigate why Educational Break is 88.62% Medical (n=167) with programme staff, for example internship or entrance-exam breaks. Decide explicitly whether Educational Break belongs with the positive, neutral or comparison group, since the YAML marks it as 'leaning positive' and asks analysts to state their choice.
  • Control for cohort when comparing streams. Report batch-stratified estimates (the 1–6 / 7–10 / 11–13 / 14–17 bands, or exposure years using Current_Age − Age_At_Workshop with both > 0). Prefer MH or flexible batch terms over a linear batch covariate, given the heterogeneity (OR 0.97 vs 0.08–0.35).
  • Keep the Medical definition fixed on Educational_Stream_Category in all future work. Do not substitute Educational_Stream, and document that Pharmacy is counted as non-Medical. If a broader 'health' definition is of interest, run it as a separate, clearly labelled analysis.
  • If extending to psychometric drivers (e.g., Pre-phase career preferences, mindset themes), join on Participant_Id as a string. How reliable is it?

All five independent analyses reached the same conclusion with identical headline counts.

  • Not a fluke: The test statistics are far beyond chance (z = −8.48, chi-square = 71.92). The 95% range for the gap is roughly −26 to −17 points, entirely below zero. In 2,000 random shuffles, none produced a gap this large.
  • Holds after adjusting for batch and gender: The odds ratio is 0.58 after adjustment and 0.39 with the batch-stratified (Mantel-Haenszel) method. Both are below 1.

Important caveats

  1. The size of the gap depends on how Students are treated.
  • Students are 1,493 of the 2,417 Non-Sustainers. They are 58.94% Medical and make up about 65% of all Medical participants.
  • Many Students may simply not have reached their outcome yet, so they may not yet be "Sustainers."
  • When Students and "Not Sure" are excluded, the Non-Sustainer Medical share drops to 35.32%. The gap shrinks to about 6.6 points and is no longer statistically significant. The adjusted odds ratio is 0.84 (95% CI 0.68–1.03), which includes 1.
  • The direction stays the same, but the strength is much less certain.
  1. Sustainers are not the lowest-Medical group. They rank 3rd lowest of 7 statuses. Other Work (19.96%) and Different Trajectory (21.95%) are lower. Sustainers are still below Previously SIW (40.40%) and the pooled Non-Sustainers.
  1. The effect is uneven across batches. Batches 1–6 show almost no difference (OR 0.97). Later batches show stronger gaps (OR about 0.08–0.35).
  1. Association only. This is observational data, and work status is an undated snapshot. Recent batches have had less time to reach Social Impact Work.

How to use this finding

  • Safe to say: "Medical-stream participants are under-represented among Sustainers compared with Non-Sustainers."
  • Avoid saying: "Medical students are less likely to sustain" or "Medical background causes people to leave." The large gap partly reflects the many still-studying Students in the comparison group.
  • If you quote a single figure, pair the −21 point gap with the note that it shrinks to about −7 points when Students and "Not Sure" are excluded.
