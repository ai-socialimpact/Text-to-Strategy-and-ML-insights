5-Run Domain Explanations (Schema Context)

Run #1: Classical Large-Sample Parametric Baseline (Pearson Chi-Square, Two-Proportion Z, Welch t, Cohen's d)

This run sets the reference numbers: 473 vs 99 participants, a 2x2 table of 136/337 vs 40/59 (Medical / Non-Medical), chi-square 5.22 (df 1) and p = 0.0224. The z-test gives z = -2.28 with the same p, because for a 2x2 table z squared equals chi-square. The OR is 0.60 (0.38 to 0.93) and the RR is 0.71 (0.54 to 0.94). The Wald CI for the difference (-22.14 to -1.16 pp) is wide because only 99 participants are Early Leavers. The age result is a mean gap of +0.75 years, with Welch p = 0.026 (t = 2.26, df = 104.6). Welch's df is far below the 386 that a pooled test would give, because the small, tighter-spread group (62 participants, SD 2.27) dominates the variance. The Mann-Whitney test gives p = 0.105 (U = 11,410). The two tests answer different questions: Welch asks about mean location, while Mann-Whitney asks about stochastic ordering of integer ages with many ties. Cohen's d = 0.26 is small. The user's rule requires both tests to be significant, so Claim 2 is INCONCLUSIVE. The run's caveat that Sustainers come from later batches (median batch 8 vs 5) previews the confounding that Run 4 quantifies.

Run #2: Exact & Rank-Based Non-Parametric Inference (Fisher Exact, Newcombe CI, Mann-Whitney with Hodges-Lehmann & Cliff's Delta)

This run checks that the Claim 1 result does not depend on large-sample approximations. Fisher's exact p is 0.031 two-sided and 0.016 one-sided, and the Yates chi-square is 4.68 (p about 0.030). These are slightly less significant than the uncorrected 0.022 because exact and continuity-corrected tests are more conservative, but they still fall below 0.05. The Newcombe interval (-22.24 to -1.58 pp) also excludes 0 and is a little more cautious than the Wald interval. In domain terms, the Medical under-representation is real in this data but fragile. The upper bound is only about 1.5 pp from zero. For age, ranks and medians show why the verdict is inconclusive. Mood's median test (p = 0.169) and Cliff's delta (0.13) both indicate a weak shift. The Hodges-Lehmann shift is +1 year while the median difference is 0.5, because integer ages quantise the shifts. The IQR is 22 to 26 for Sustainers and 22 to 24 for Early Leavers. The Sustainers' wider upper tail (ages up to 31) is what lifts the mean without moving the median much. The rank tests do not confirm a robust age difference.

Run #3: Seeded Bootstrap Confidence Intervals & Permutation Tests

These resampling methods assume nothing about the data distribution and agree with the analytic tests on Claim 1. The permutation p is about 0.022, and the bootstrap CI for the difference is -22.18 to -1.36 pp (OR 0.38 to 0.94, RR 0.55 to 0.96, SE 5.35 pp). On the age claim, the mean permutation p is about 0.06, which is looser than Welch's 0.026. This is expected. A label-shuffling test assumes the two groups have the same distribution, including the same spread. Here the SDs differ (2.95 vs 2.27) and the group sizes are very unequal (326 vs 62). The permutation test therefore behaves like a pooled-variance test, and a pooled-variance t-test would also give p of roughly 0.06. Welch's test is more sensitive here because it treats the Early Leavers' tight spread as real information. The median permutation p is about 0.56, and the bootstrap CI for the median difference (-0.5 to 1) spans 0. Median_S > median_E in only about 51% of resamples, while mean_S > mean_E in about 99%. The age signal lives in the Sustainers' upper tail, not in the middle of the distribution. The result is highly method-dependent, which is why Claim 2 stays INCONCLUSIVE.

Run #4: Cohort-Stratified Mantel-Haenszel & Batch-Weighted Comparison (Censoring Sensitivity)

This run directly tests the schema's censoring rule: recent Workshop_Batch_Number cohorts have had less time to realise outcomes, so batch must be controlled. The crude results match the other runs. The batch crosstab shows severe imbalance. Sustainers are 63% of batch 9 but 92 to 96% of several others, and 100% of batches 14, 16 and 17, which have no Early Leavers. A recent participant cannot yet be a 'Previously SIW', so those batches inflate the Sustainers count with participants who have not yet been tested by time. For Claim 1, the stratified analysis (14 strata, 407 Sustainers and 99 Early Leavers) keeps the direction but loses significance. MH OR is 0.71 (0.45 to 1.14), MH RR is 0.81, and CMH p is 0.18. Breslow-Day shows strong heterogeneity (p < 0.01), with stratum ORs from about 0.04 to 5.78 and several sparse strata. A single pooled effect is therefore a weak summary, and the crude Medical effect is better described as fragile than as refuted. For Claim 2, only 6 batches had both groups with at least 2 valid ages. That is an effective N of 213 vs 59, and many participants fall out because of the age-0 unknowns. The pooled difference reverses to -0.22 (p = 0.50, van Elteren p = 0.44). The crude age gap therefore looks like a batch-composition artefact. One caution is that the 6-stratum adjusted age analysis is much smaller and less representative than the crude one. Also, the Claim 1 refined-query wording in this run read 'Sustainers higher'. That contradicts the user's claim, and the run's narrative flagged the inconsistency, but the verdict rule used was the correct literal one.

Run #5: Model-Based GLM Estimation with Robust SEs plus Yuen 20% Trimmed-Mean Test

Regression framing reproduces the same facts and adds model-based confirmation. The logistic regression on a single binary group term is saturated, so exp(beta) equals the 2x2 cross-product OR of 0.60 (beta about -0.52, SE 0.23, Wald p = 0.02). The LR chi-square (5.02, p about 0.025) differs slightly from Pearson's 5.22, as the two tests normally do. The modified Poisson RR is 0.71. The linear-probability risk difference is -11.65 pp, with HC3 p = 0.031 and an upper CI bound about 1 pp from zero. Each metric is a different expression of the same 2x2 table, so agreement is expected and is not independent evidence. For age, OLS with HC3 (p about 0.025) is the regression equivalent of Welch's test. Yuen's 20% trimmed test (difference 0.72, p = 0.096) removes the extreme ages and also fails significance. Trimming mimics the rank tests, which supports the idea that the age difference is driven by a few older Sustainers (ages up to 31). The 5% trimmed difference stays at 0.74. Hedges' g is 0.26, the same as d. Mean-based and trimmed or rank-based estimators diverge, and that divergence is the essential reason for the INCONCLUSIVE verdict.

Theoretical Framework & Statistical Invariance

Claim 1: every method puts the Medical share lower among Sustainers (28.75% vs 40.40%, -11.65 pp). P-values range from 0.016 to 0.031, depending on whether the test is uncorrected chi-square, z, Fisher, Yates, permutation, logistic LR, robust Poisson or LPM. Every interval for the difference, OR and RR excludes the null, but only just: the upper bounds are about -1 pp for the difference and 0.93–0.96 for the ratios. The smaller group (n=99) makes the estimate imprecise. Claim 2: after dropping unknown ages (147 Sustainers, 37 Early Leavers; effective n 326 vs 62), Sustainers are higher on mean (+0.75) and median (+0.5), with a small effect (d≈0.26). Tests that weight the mean are borderline significant: Welch p≈0.026, OLS-HC3 p≈0.025 and a bootstrap CI of 0.09–1.39. Tests that are robust to the shape or spread of the distribution are not: Mann-Whitney p≈0.105, Yuen 20% trimmed p≈0.096, Mood's median p≈0.169, median permutation p≈0.56, and a bootstrap median-difference CI that spans 0. The user's rule requires both Welch and Mann-Whitney to be significant, so the verdict is inconclusive. Early Leavers have a much smaller spread (SD 2.27 vs 2.95), which helps explain the mean-versus-rank gap. Run 4's batch-stratified sensitivity analysis shows that batch is a plausible confounder. The groups are very unevenly spread across batches: batches 14, 16 and 17 have no Early Leavers, and recent batches are right-censored. After adjustment, the Medical association keeps its direction but loses significance (MH OR 0.71, CMH p=0.18, with strong Breslow-Day heterogeneity). The age difference reverses (pooled -0.22, p=0.50, using only 6 strata). All results are associations in single-arm observational data. CurrentStatusOf_Work is an undated snapshot, and no multiplicity correction was applied.

Caveats on how far the result holds

  • Robust across methods. Pearson, z, Fisher (p ≈ 0.031), permutation (p ≈ 0.022), logistic LR (p ≈ 0.025) and robust Poisson all give the same direction and significance at α = 0.05. However, the CI upper bounds are close to the null (about −1 pp for the difference and 0.93–0.96 for the ratios), and the Early Leaver group is small (n = 99).
  • Weakens after batch adjustment. In Run 4, stratifying by WorkshopBatchNumber kept the direction but lost significance. The Mantel-Haenszel OR was 0.71 (0.45–1.14) with CMH p = 0.18, and the Breslow-Day test showed strong heterogeneity. Batches 14, 16 and 17 have no Early Leavers, and recent batches are right-censored. The crude result is therefore best described as fragile to cohort composition.
  • Wording of the verdict rule. Run 4 noted that the verdict rule can be read inconsistently, but the data clearly show a lower Medical share among Sustainers.
  • Association only. This is single-arm observational data with an undated status snapshot. It is not evidence that the workshop caused the difference, and no multiplicity correction was applied.
